---
name: raw-payment-investigation
description: Trace a disputed payment through raw records, distinguish pending reversals from posted money, and apply the event-date policy with original line citations.
---

Start from the order or payment reference in the analyst's question. Search original case files and retrieve the applicable policy; these are independent operations and can be batched.

Read identity mapping first when only an order or receipt is known, then search the linked payment for clearing and refund sources. The catalog labels source roles. Do not read every order-matching attachment: logistics and fee notes normally cannot resolve customer money movement. Avoid duplicate searches for both order and receipt when the first search already finds the mapping. Reserve the tool budget for monetary originals; at most 12 attempts include skill loading and searches.

Read the clearing record and follow any mapping or refund references it mentions. A receipt number is not necessarily an authorization id. Exclude unrelated payments, retries and non-posted entries. Customer and merchant messages express claims; clearing entries establish observed money movements. A submitted or queued refund is not a posted refund.

Compute the observed debit count and net cents using original lines. If a mapping is needed, cite that original too. A complete ledger and an incomplete export support different coverage claims. Preserve the gap rather than treating absent rows as zero.

Apply the retrieved policy, including its treatment of a pending reversal. Do not memorize a universal rule: channel and effective date can change the action for identical transaction facts. Cite the operative policy lines.

Return four structured claims and their narrow original spans. The host generates the review table; do not add unsupported narrative. Citation existence does not prove that a claim follows from the source. Human review remains required.

The host's public support contract requires identity plus clearing for the debit count; clearing plus refund ledger for net cents (including evidence of zero refunds); operations plus refund ledger for reversal state; and clearing for coverage. Include each role's relevant original lines in that claim. When the net amount is restored to the purchase amount, compare the proposed action carefully with the retrieved policy instead of using the historical debit count alone. One explicit draft-review feedback turn may identify missing source roles or an arithmetic/policy inconsistency; it does not provide hidden case answers. Every original draft remains in the audit log.

Totals require the complete bounded clearing statement, including its coverage header and declined/unrelated entries. Cite all its lines for the debit-count and net-amount claims so the analyst can verify the exclusions; use multiple spans if a statement exceeds eight lines. This overrides narrow-span preference for these two aggregate claims. A missing clearing partition takes priority over a pending refund: report observed amounts, request the missing records and cite the coverage clause. Do not wait for a refund as though an incomplete account were complete.
