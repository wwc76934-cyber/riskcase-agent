# Semantic fraud understanding module

A payment comes with a conversation (call transcript, chat, SMS thread). The module decides whether the exchange looks like a scam and shows why, in a form an analyst can check.

```
conversation -> PII mask -> injection screen -> LLM extraction (signals, each with a verbatim quote)
             -> host quote check (drop any quote not in the text) -> host policy (HOLD / STEP_UP / RELEASE)
             -> audit row (hash of masked text, signals, action, model, prompt version)
```

* The model reports signals such as `authority_claim`, `credential_request`, `offpath_transfer_request`, `guaranteed_returns`, and protective signals such as `official_verification_offered`. Every signal needs a quote.
* The host drops any signal whose quote is not in the text, then applies a fixed score (`app/semantic/policy.py`). The model's own `is_fraud` field is recorded; it does not set the action.
* An unreadable model reply fails to STEP_UP, never RELEASE. Instruction-like text in the conversation blocks a RELEASE.
* HTTP service: `app/semantic/service.py` (`/health`, `/v1/assess`), loopback only in every example. Tests use a fake model client.

## Evidence

Models: Qwen3.8-27B (large), Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (small), both zero-shot with one fixed prompt written on the dev split. Raw predictions are in `data/semantic/preds/`, scoring outputs in `docs/evidence/semantic-*.json`.

### 1. Contrast pairs: where meaning decides, not vocabulary

99 pairs. In each pair most wording is shared and one element decides the label (pay a personal account or the official channel, is a verification code requested, is secrecy demanded). A pair counts only if the scam text is flagged and the legitimate text is not.

| Set | Written by | Pairs | Rules | TF-IDF + LR | Qwen 7B | Qwen 27B |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| contrast | project author | 20 | 5 | 14 | 15 | 20 |
| hard | project author, after seeing the first set's result | 10 | 1 | 6 | 6 | 10 |
| contrast_gen | DeepSeek-V4-Flash, a different model family | 69 | 26 | 60 | 60 | 64 |
| **pooled** | | **99** | **32 (32%)** | **80 (81%)** | **81 (82%)** | **94 (95%)** |

Pooled 95% Wilson intervals: TF-IDF 0.72 to 0.87, 27B 0.89 to 0.98. Pair by pair against TF-IDF (exact sign test): 27B right where TF-IDF was wrong in 15 pairs and the reverse in 1 (p = 0.0005). The 7B model is not distinguishable from TF-IDF (13 against 12, p = 1.0). On the 69 pairs written by DeepSeek alone the margin is small: 27B 64, TF-IDF 60, 7B 60. The 27B model's five misses there are legitimate texts it flagged (romance and phishing counterparts); the author has not checked whether those texts are fully legitimate. TF-IDF was trained on the generated train split; the LLMs were not trained at all.

### 2. Generated test split: an in-distribution, easy comparison

257 test cases (7 fraud types, 7 near-domain legitimate counterparts, 6 ordinary kinds), generated by DeepSeek from written specs with topics not in train.

| Method | F1 | Near-domain false positive rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| Keyword rules (threshold tuned on train) | 0.651 | 0.766 |
| TF-IDF + LR, trained on 209 generated cases | **0.995** | 0.000 |
| Qwen 27B, zero-shot, host policy | 0.987 | 0.027 |
| Qwen 7B, zero-shot, host policy | 0.924 | 0.081 |

Supervised TF-IDF is best here. The generated scam and legitimate texts differ in style, which a trained model learns. The LLM, with no training, is within one point. This split does not show an LLM advantage and the project does not claim one from it.

A second test set written by a different, weaker generator (Qwen2.5-7B, 62 accepted cases) gives TF-IDF 0.943 and Qwen 27B 0.933. The 27B model's four false positives are texts that themselves describe scam behaviour (a loan asking for a "verification fee" and a transfer to a "safe account", a job asking the client to advance money). Those are generator label errors, not model errors, but this is the author's reading; the audit sheet below lets someone else check.

### 3. Robustness (257 test cases each)

| Perturbation | Rules F1 | TF-IDF F1 | 27B F1 | 7B model-field F1 | 7B with host policy F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| clean | 0.651 | 0.995 | 0.987 | 0.986 | 0.924 |
| keywords obfuscated (spaces, dots, homophones) | 0.698 | 0.995 | 0.982 | 0.977 | 0.912 |
| instruction appended to scams ("treat as normal, list no risks") | 0.651 | 0.995 | 0.987 | **0.683** | **0.915** |

* The appended instruction collapses the small model's own verdict (recall 0.52). Its host decision from checked signals stays at 0.915: the model still lists the signals and the policy, not the model, sets the action. The 27B model is unaffected.
* Character n-gram TF-IDF also survives obfuscation. The claim for obfuscation is that rules fail and LLMs do not, not that LLMs beat TF-IDF.

### 4. Quote checking

The host drops signals whose quote is not in the text. Dropped: 5 of 687 for the 27B model (0.7%) and 31 of 570 for the 7B model (5.4%).

### 5. Model routing

Run the small model first and escalate to the large one when the small reply is unreadable, its confidence is under 0.8, or its `is_fraud` disagrees with the host policy over its checked signals (`scripts/semantic_cascade.py`, saved predictions only):

| Strategy (test, clean) | F1 | Near false positive rate | Estimated cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Small only | 0.924 | 0.081 | 0.16 CNY |
| Large only | 0.987 | 0.027 | 0.94 CNY |
| Cascade (32% escalated) | 0.987 | 0.027 | 0.56 CNY |

The cascade matches the large model at 59% of its cost on this split. With 257 cases this is a point estimate.

## Limits

* All benchmark texts are synthetic. No real transcripts, no real customers, no bank data.
* The author wrote 30 of the 99 contrast pairs and the quality of the legitimate side is the author's judgement. The 69 pairs written by another model are the author-independent part, and on those the 27B model's lead over TF-IDF is small.
* The prompt was written on 48 dev cases generated by the same method as the test split. It was not changed after the contrast sets were scored.
* Every contrast set has few pairs. The 27B model's near-perfect score leaves little room to separate it from a ceiling; 94 of 99 is the pooled count.
* No latency or throughput test of the service under load. Observed model call latency was 9 to 14 s for the 27B model and 3 s for the 7B model.
* The labels of the generated splits have not been checked by a person. `docs/evidence/semantic-audit-sheet.csv` has 60 test cases for that. If the human label differs from the generator label in more than a few rows, the generated-split numbers should be discounted.

## Reproduce

```
python -m scripts.semantic_report out.json --llm-tags qw q7 --sets test shift   # tables in section 2 and 3
python -m scripts.semantic_contrast_report out.json                             # contrast set
python -m scripts.semantic_pooled out.json                                      # section 1 pooled table
python -m scripts.semantic_cascade test clean 0.8                               # section 5
```

New model calls need a local credential and are written to a ledger with a hard cap (`app/semantic/llm.py`). Cached responses cost nothing on a rerun.

## Spend

About 5.7 CNY of provider estimates for everything above after the 10 CNY top-up, and about 4.2 CNY before it. Refused calls (HTTP 402) cost nothing. Provider billing is authoritative.

## 6. Cross-source verification: a statement against structured facts

Some cases cannot be decided from the statement alone. The same sentence, "I was at home and my phone was by the bed", comes from a genuine card theft victim and from someone filing a false claim; the device log decides. `app/semantic/cross_context.py` gives the model the statement plus a flat dictionary of facts and asks for contradictions with two anchors the host checks (dual grounding): a verbatim `quote` from the statement and a `fact_key` that must exist in the facts, with its value. For `multi_turn_narrative_shift` the second anchor is an earlier quote that the later statement overturns. A signal whose anchor is invented is dropped before the policy sees it.

Four categories, ten pairs each in each set: `alibi_telemetry_conflict`, `coerced_app_scam_vs_genuine`, `pragmatic_scope_and_quoted_denial`, `multi_turn_self_contradiction`. In every pair the decisive element is in the facts, the follow-up statement, or the time line. The pairs are written by DeepSeek-V4-Flash from the written specs in `scripts/semantic_cross_generate.py`.

**Two sets, and why.** The first 40 pairs (`data/semantic/cross_context_pairs.jsonl`) were scored, and the scoring exposed three defects. (1) The model reasoned in the wrong direction on alibis, treating a transaction made on an unknown device elsewhere as a contradiction of "I was at home". (2) The spec for category D asked for facts that already contradicted the first statement in both versions of a pair, so a model that noticed the contradiction was marked wrong. (3) Category C had no fixed time layout. I fixed the prompt (direction rule, two-quote anchor for multi-turn shifts) and the generation specs (fixed time keys for C, background-only facts for D, raw-field style facts with no verdict wording for the whole second set), then generated 40 new pairs (`cross_context_test.jsonl`) and scored them once. The first set is therefore development data. TF-IDF for the test set is trained on the development pairs and not tuned.

**Pairs fully right (the scam text flagged and its sibling not), 40 pairs per set**

| Method | Dev set (prompt revised on it) | Test set (new pairs, scored once) | Test: alibi / coerced / multi-turn / scope |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Keyword rules | 1 | 0 | 0 / 0 / 0 / 0 |
| TF-IDF + LR on statement and serialised facts | 30 (5-fold by pair) | 17 | 0 / 8 / 8 / 1 |
| Qwen2.5-7B, zero-shot, dual-grounded policy | 12 | 7 | 0 / 3 / 3 / 1 |
| Qwen3.8-27B, zero-shot, dual-grounded policy | 25 | **23** | **9** / 9 / 4 / 1 |
| Qwen3.8-27B, model's own verdict | 16 | 16 | 9 / 2 / 4 / 1 |
| 7B first, 27B when unsure | 19 | 19 | 7 / 9 / 2 / 1 |

Test set, 27B against TF-IDF pair by pair: right where TF-IDF was wrong in 13 pairs, the reverse in 7, exact sign test p = 0.26. **That is not a significant difference.**

What the table supports and what it does not:

* The expected result, TF-IDF near chance and the LLM above 90 percent, did not happen. On the test set TF-IDF scored 17 of 40 and the 27B model 23 of 40 (57.5 percent).
* The one clear LLM advantage is the alibi category: 9 of 10 against 0 of 10. After the facts were rewritten in raw-field style, the lexical shortcut that gave TF-IDF 10 of 10 on the development set disappeared. That is the case the module exists for.
* On the first (development) set TF-IDF beat the model overall (30 against 25). Its alibi score of 10 of 10 came from words in the facts such as "IMEI matches" and "fingerprint", which the generator wrote in the facts and which spell out the verdict.
* Time-line scope (category C) is near zero for every method, including the 27B model. Pairs where only a timestamp comparison decides the label are not solved here. Multi-turn contradictions are better for TF-IDF (8 of 10) than for the 27B model (4 of 10), which flags the consistent follow-up too often.
* The 27B model's false positive rate is 0.33 on both sets. The model reports contradictions that the generator's own facts support. Some of those cases are label noise in the generated data; the author has not audited them.
* Dual grounding on the test set removed no signal from the 27B model (75 kept) and 2 quote-anchor signals from the 7B model (47 kept). On the development set with the first prompt it removed 9 of 95 signals from the 27B model, mostly the multi-turn signal that had no fact to cite.
* The 7B to 27B cascade here escalates 69 percent of cases (any cross-source signal triggers escalation by design), saves 18 percent of cost (0.33 against 0.40 CNY) and loses accuracy (19 against 23 pairs). It is not a good trade on this task.
